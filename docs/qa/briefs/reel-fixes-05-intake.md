## 1. The caves' lamps (T-69) — `sandbox/caves-descent/props.ts` (the `LAMPS` table and the hook loop); `sandbox/_shared/lantern.ts` only if the probe says so

Three screenshots: "floating lamp stand 1 / 2" and "the lower you traverse the lamp lights themselves float higher and higher above the lamps — some lamp stands are also just floating".

Diagnosis, read from the source: the fourteen hook-lamps are hung at **typed heights** (`LAMPS` is `[x, z, y]` with y "hung at 2 m over the ground", and the post is drawn at `gy − 1.6`); round 2 moved the ground under them (the relief's ±0.54 m on the tiers; the stair's treads now level steps rather than a ramp), so a post whose foot was at the old ground now floats, and the comment at line ~33 says the hook-lamps "keep their built" heights across the `J` rebuild. That explains the floating stands. It does **not** by itself explain the glow rising above the cage with depth. Two candidates the builder must measure, not guess: (a) a part of the lantern (its glass, flame or glow quad) on a material without the curved-world bend, so it separates from the bent cage by `0.0006 · d²` (T-41's rule: 0.54 m at 30 m, 0.96 m at 40 m — "higher the lower you go" is "further from the curve centre"); (b) the lantern's pivot placed at `gy` while the hook's cage is drawn relative to a different y. A probe reads, for four lamps at increasing depth, the world y of the cage's top, the glass and the light, and the distance from the curve centre; the numbers name the cause.

Fix: every hook-lamp's post stands on `groundY(x, z)` sampled **after** the relief is built and re-sampled on every `J` rebuild (extend `reground()`); the lantern hangs 2.1 m over that ground. If (a): the offending material takes the world bend (the T-41 rule, as the ring did). Check: for all fourteen lamps at all three relief settings, post foot within 0.03 m of `groundY`, glass within 0.05 m of the cage's hook point, at every depth; a frame from the S2 station and one from the Depths floor looking back up (the deepest lamps).
