## What and why

Andrew and his son looked at round 1 (`docs/reference/Screenshots/Crystal Caverns - *.jpg`). They like that the ring lies on the floor and the stair joins the tier. They **miss the look of the reel-2 build**: "the chunky floor" (columns at staggered heights, faceted caps catching the lamp light, the floor reading as geometry) and "the way the stairs looked before, the blockyness and the colors" (stacked box steps, alternating light and dark). And the "flying crystal creature" is a salamander hanging in the air: its typed wall point belongs to the stair round 1 rebuilt.

The principle from round 1 stands and is the whole trick: **what is walked is what is drawn.** Round 1 got there by flattening the drawn surface to the walked one. This round goes the other way: the drawn surface gets its relief back and `groundY` reads its height *from the drawn columns and steps*.

## The fixes

1. **T-61a, the relief.** Every tier column gets a quantized top: `top = TIER_TOP[t] + relief(x, z)` where `relief` is a deterministic function of the column's grid cell, clustered (use `noise(x/6, z/6)` plus a per-cell hash), quantized to a 0.18 m grid, in {−0.36, −0.18, 0, +0.18, +0.36, +0.54} with about half the columns at 0 and no adjacent pair differing by more than 0.54 m (the walk's `maxStep` is 1.1). Keep the column radius, the xz jitter (≤ 0.08 m), the random rotation, the `#4A4270` caps, the colour split. **`groundY` on a tier returns the highest drawn column top under the point**: keep a list of the columns (centre, rotation, top radius, top) and test the point against the top hexagon of each column within one grid pitch (a point-in-hexagon test in the column's own frame). A point on a tier that no column covers is a bug: assert it in the test. The stair's cut (`cutAt`, `lowTread`, the shoulder) works as before, on the relieved tops.
2. **T-61b, the blocky stair.** Group the 0.15 m cross-sections into **steps of 0.60 m of arc**; each step is one level tread at the ramp's height at the step's middle, so `stairY` returns a staircase (a riser of about 0.20 m on the first stair). Draw each step as the old build did: a box the full band width (plus 0.3 m overhang each side where it is not cut into a tier), 0.5 m tall or down to the next tread, colour alternating **per step** between `K.rockLight` and `#4A4270` with `jitterColor` ±0.05, the tread top exactly at the step's `stairY`. Keep the channel wall and shoulder logic, the flared mouth and the apron. The kids then walk a real stair: the walk probe from round 1 (`scripts/probes/caves-stair.cjs`) must still descend with every step ≤ `maxStep`.
3. **T-61c, the ring conforms** (`_shared/rig.ts`). The ring is a `RingGeometry(0.45, 0.84, 48)` at +0.04 with a curve-bending vertex shader. Give it a per-vertex `aLift` attribute (one float per vertex) that the vertex shader adds to world y after the curve term, and update it each frame the kid has moved more than 0.02 m: `aLift[i] = ground(worldX_i, worldZ_i) − root.y`, clamped to [−0.6, +0.8], with `ground` the scene's `groundY` the rig already receives for `groundRel`. Cost: 98 `groundY` calls per moved kid per frame; measure it (`performance.now()` around the update, quote the mean over 120 frames; must be under 0.5 ms for four kids). The ring's own centre stays at +0.04 over the kid's ground. Scenes with flat ground see `aLift = 0` everywhere: byte-identical frames there are the check.
4. **T-62, the salamanders cling.** Place each of the two by a **cast**, not a typed point: choose an arc position on the first stair where it is cut into the Landing (the channel wall exists there: `lowTread(x, z) !== null` and the tier top is above the tread), cast horizontally from the stair's centreline along the cross-section normal against the `stairs` mesh (the wall), and put the salamander at the hit with its belly on the wall (rotate the root so its local −y is the wall's normal and its local +x runs along the wall, head up-stair), 0.5–0.9 m above the tread. The second on the second stair the same way, or on a column's side face at the crescent's edge if the second stair has no cut there (say which). The **climb** (the 3 s still → 1 m closer behaviour) moves along the wall's surface: re-cast every step so the belly stays on the rock. Keep the lerp rate (0.8/s): Andrew liked the slow drift. The `still`/`climb` logic is otherwise unchanged.
5. **The gameplay idea, logged not built.** Append one row to `docs/design/PRE_BUILD_TODO.md` (next P-nn): Andrew's idea that the chunkier caves floor could become traversal — a maze with few exits, or paths only a jump reaches, or a spell that lifts the kids where they cannot jump; gameplay for the story lane, not this round.
6. **Round-1 deferrals.** (a) Move the three hook-lamps that stand inside the first stair's band (`props.ts`: `[-20,-31]`, `[-27,-25]`, `[-33,-17]`) sideways along their cross-section normal to 0.4 m outside the band's edge, at the same arc. (b) At the second stair's foot, blend `floorY` to the stair's last tread over 3 m (a smoothstep on distance from the foot) so the lip is ≤ 0.15 m.
7. **The key.** `J` cycles the relief: `flat` (round 1's floor and swept stair, kept as a variant for another layout), `chunky` (this round's default: the relief and the blocky stair), `blocks` (the same with the relief doubled: amplitude ×2, still ≤ `maxStep`). `?relief=flat|chunky|blocks` on the URL sets it for a frame. The HUD line names the setting. Rebuild the tiers, the stair and `groundY`'s column list on the change; the kids are re-grounded (`root.position.y = groundY`) so nobody is left inside rock. Verify `J` is unbound in `_shared/scene.ts` before using it (T, V, K, P, F, U, O, B, X, H, Tab, Enter, comma and period are the runtime's).

## Acceptance checks (quote the numbers)

1. **`caves.test.ts`** (trim first, Working Rule 7; the suite has 39 tests, add ≤ 5): (a) 400 seeded points on the Landing and the gallery ledge: a raycast down against `tiers` agrees with `groundY` within **0.05 m**, and every point is covered by at least one column; (b) along both stairs' centrelines and ±1.0 m, 0.25 m spacing: `groundY` is a staircase — every rise is 0 or between 0.12 and 0.30 m, never negative going down, and the highest `stairs`/`tiers` hit is within 0.05 m of `stairY`; (c) 300 points on the tiers: `|groundY(p) − groundY(q)| ≤ 0.54` for every q within 2.2 m of p (no step exceeds the limit); (d) the relief histogram: at `chunky`, between 35 % and 65 % of columns at 0 and every quantum represented.
